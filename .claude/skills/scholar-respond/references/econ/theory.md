# Theory

Routed to **R2**, and read whenever the paper carries a formal result — including an applied paper whose proposition does real work. Principles that bite hardest: **P1**, **P6**, **P7**.

No other reference tests whether a proof is valid. That is why this dimension survives as its own.

Prefix findings `TH-`.

## Check 1 — Assumption completeness and importance

**Tests** whether every assumption a proposition, lemma or comparative static needs is stated and economically interpretable. **Violation**: a result rests on an unstated, hidden or implausibly strong assumption that the theorem statement and its surrounding text do not flag. **Evidence required**: the theorem, proposition or equation, and the assumption that is missing or implicit.

## Check 2 — Proof correctness

**Tests** whether each step follows from the stated assumptions, and whether an example is quietly doing proof work. **Violation**: a proof skips a nontrivial step, uses a condition stronger than the one stated, fails at a boundary case, or proves something weaker than the claim. **Evidence required**: the proof step, equation, appendix location — or the counterexample.

## Check 3 — Generality and scope

**Tests** whether the domain over which the result holds is stated, and general theorems are kept apart from special cases. **Violation**: a result is presented as general while depending on a particular functional form, an equilibrium selection, a distributional assumption or a parameter restriction. **Evidence required**: the result statement and the restrictive condition.

## Check 4 — Novelty and recasting

**Tests** whether the formal contribution is more than notation, relabelling, or a direct corollary of a known result. **Violation**: the claimed novelty is a recasting of an existing theorem or mechanism with no new insight, application or comparative static. **Evidence required**: the novelty claim and the paper's own comparison to prior work.

## Check 5 — Economic interpretation

**Tests** whether the theorem delivers interpretable economics rather than mathematical possibility. **Violation**: central formal objects, welfare implications or comparative statics are never mapped back to behaviour or observables. **Evidence required**: the result and the missing interpretation.

## Invariants

- Do not demand empirical validation from a theory paper unless the paper makes empirical claims.
- An optional model extension is not a finding unless the current theorem fails on its own terms.
- Calibrate: a proof error or a false proposition is CRITICAL; a scope clarification is usually MAJOR or MINOR.
