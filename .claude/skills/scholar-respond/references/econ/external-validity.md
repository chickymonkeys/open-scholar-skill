# External Validity

Routed to **R2**. Principle that bites hardest: **P6** — a causal estimate identifies an effect for
one population, one margin and one dose; carrying it anywhere else is a separate claim needing its
own argument.

Identification asks whether the estimate is recovered inside the study. This reference asks whether
it transports.

Prefix findings `EV-`.

## Check 1 — Scope statement

**Tests** whether the paper states the population, geography and period its results apply to.
**Violation**: results are presented with no explicit scope, leaving the applicable population
ambiguous.
**Evidence required**: the section where scope is or is not stated.

## Check 2 — Generalisability claims

**Tests** whether extrapolation to other countries, periods or populations rests on an argument.
**Violation**: generalisability is asserted beyond the study sample with no stated mechanism or
supporting evidence.
**Evidence required**: the sentence making the claim; what makes it unsupported.

## Check 3 — Institutional and regulatory context

**Tests** whether the institutional setting is described well enough for a reader to judge transport.
**Violation**: the context is not described and the results turn on context-specific rules that may
not hold elsewhere.
**Evidence required**: the section describing context; what is missing from it.

## Check 4 — Temporal limits

**Tests** whether period-specific findings are bounded as such.
**Violation**: results from a particular macroeconomic or policy era are presented with no
acknowledgement that they may not hold in another.
**Evidence required**: the period; the absence of a temporal caveat.

## Check 5 — LATE against ATE

**Tests** whether IV and RDD papers acknowledge that the estimate is local.
**Violation**: an IV or RDD paper never acknowledges the distinction anywhere.
**Evidence required**: confirm the absence by citing the identification section and the conclusion.

## Check 6 — Dose and intensity

**Tests** whether the estimated effect is read at the dose the study actually delivered.
**Violation**: a dose-specific effect is interpreted as applying at a different intensity with no
justification.
**Evidence required**: the treatment dose described; the interpretation that departs from it.

## What the field tunes

The field decides which institutions and eligibility rules a reader must be shown before transport
can be judged — a development RCT's implementing organisation, a labour paper's benefit schedule, a
public-finance paper's tax code, an IO paper's market structure. Name the institutional facts this
field requires and check that the paper supplies them.

## Invariants

- Most external-validity limitations are inherent to empirical work. Rating them CRITICAL is
  overcalibrated unless the limitation is central to the contribution.
- The LATE/ATE distinction is often acknowledged implicitly in the identification section. Read
  carefully before flagging absence.
