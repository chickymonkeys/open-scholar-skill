# Mechanism

Routed to **R2**, and read only when the paper claims a channel. Principles that bite hardest: **P1** and **P6**.

The question is whether the paper separates the average effect it identifies from the channel it says explains that effect.

Prefix findings `MECH-`.

## Check 1 — Mechanism claim against average effect

**Tests** whether a channel is inferred from an average effect with no independent evidence. **Violation**: the abstract, introduction or conclusion claims a channel or a behavioural response while the paper estimates only a reduced-form average. **Evidence required**: the mechanism claim; the results table, or the absence of mechanism evidence.

## Check 2 — Mechanism-specific evidence

**Tests** whether the proposed channel is tested with outcomes, margins, timing or heterogeneity that the channel — and not its rivals — predicts. **Violation**: mechanism evidence is absent, misaligned with the claimed channel, or equally consistent with a rival channel — whether the paper leaves that rival unnamed **or names several and separates none**. A paper that endorses three interpretations at once and reports evidence consistent with all of them has not tested a mechanism; the binding question is which test would have come out differently under each. **Evidence required**: the mechanism table or heterogeneity analysis, or the missing test.

## Check 3 — Heterogeneity read as mechanism

**Tests** whether subgroup differences are tied to an estimand and to a multiple-testing context. **Violation**: subgroup differences are treated as mechanism evidence with no account of the subgroup estimand, how subgroups were chosen, the power available, or the number of splits tried. **Evidence required**: the heterogeneity table and the claim language.

## Check 4 — Mechanism used to travel

**Tests** whether a local channel is used to justify claims beyond the studied population or setting. **Violation**: a mechanism result from one context supports a broad policy or welfare claim with no bridge argument. **Evidence required**: the mechanism evidence and the extrapolating statement. The scope half of this belongs to `external-validity.md`; keep the finding on whichever side the paper's claim sits.

## Invariants

- Do not demand a mechanism from a paper that explicitly confines itself to a well-defined reduced-form effect.
- A mechanism finding binds only where mechanism language carries the contribution, the interpretation, a policy claim or an external-validity claim.
- Suggest a mechanism test only where the paper's own data can run it.
