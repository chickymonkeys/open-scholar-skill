# Contribution

Routed to **R3**. Principles that bite hardest: **P1** (the contribution claim must rest on a quantity the design identifies) and **P6** (a mechanism story is a separate claim from the estimate).

A valid estimate is not automatically a contribution. This is the editorially decisive dimension and it is read for every economics paper, whatever its form.

Prefix findings `CONTRIB-`.

## Check 1 — Novelty against cited work

**Tests** whether the paper distinguishes itself from the prior work it cites most directly. **Violation**: the stated contribution is not distinguished from a directly cited paper — same design, same kind of data, same question, same population — with no stated increment. **Evidence required**: the prior work; the sentence claiming distinction; what the distinction is and why it does not hold.

## Check 2 — Novelty against uncited work

**Tests** whether methodologically close work the paper does not cite would force the novelty claim to be sharpened. **Violation**: a directly relevant paper exists, is uncited, and would require the authors to differentiate. **Evidence required**: the citation, and why it is directly relevant. Raise this only where the work is known to exist; say so plainly if the recollection is uncertain rather than asserting a paper.

## Check 3 — Claim–evidence alignment

**Tests** whether the causal language of the abstract and conclusion is supported by the design. **Violation**: the abstract or conclusion says "causes", "leads to", "increases" for a result the identification strategy does not deliver causally. **Evidence required**: the sentence with page; the design; why it does not support the claim. Under P1: if the abstract claims "the effect on X" while the design recovers a LATE for a narrow complier group, the contribution is narrower than stated, and that gap is the finding.

## Check 4 — Causal language attached to a derived or aggregate quantity

**Tests** whether the discussion, the policy implications and any counterfactual or simulation exercise keep to what the design supports. Check 3 covers the paper's estimates; this check covers what the paper builds *on top of* them — an aggregate share, a simulated policy, a projection. **Violation**: a derived quantity is described causally while the section that constructs it says it identifies something narrower; or policy implications carry causal language with no credible identification behind them. **Evidence required**: the sentence and its page; the passage constructing the quantity; the gap between what that passage claims to identify and what the sentence asserts.

## Check 5 — Headline numbers against the tables

**Tests** whether the paper's headline quantitative claims match the body — **wherever those claims appear**. The abstract is the first place to look and often carries no numbers at all; the claims then live in the last third of the introduction and the first paragraph of the conclusion, which is where a working paper usually puts them. **Violation**: a coefficient, percentage, ratio or p-value in a headline claim does not match the corresponding number in the text or the tables, or cannot be reconstructed from them. **Evidence required**: the headline number with its location, and the table number that disagrees or the reconstruction that fails. Where a headline number does reconcile, say so and show the arithmetic.

## Check 6 — Caveat acknowledgement

**Tests** whether the standard limitation of the chosen design is acknowledged anywhere. **Violation**: a standard limitation — the LATE/ATE distinction for IV and RDD, the local nature of a discontinuity estimate — appears nowhere in the paper. **Evidence required**: name the limitation and confirm its absence.

## What the field tunes

The economic field decides which prior estimates the contribution is read beside and what a comparable claim looks like there. Name the comparator literature the paper should be measured against in this field, and say why it is the comparator.

## Invariants

- "I would frame the contribution differently" is not a finding. Name the prior work that makes the claim non-novel.
- Causal language inside a clearly descriptive section is not a CRITICAL finding.
- Caveats may live in appendices or online supplements. Read them before flagging absence.
